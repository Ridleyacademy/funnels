Why notation-first stalls adult learners

Reading music is a beautiful skill. Making it the gate you must pass before touching real music is the mistake.

🎼

It front-loads the driest part

Staff lines, note names, and counting drills before a single song you'd choose to play. Adults don't have patience for homework with no payoff, and they shouldn't. That order kills the momentum you walked in with.

🧩

It hides the patterns

Real songs run on chords, progressions, and shapes that repeat across all of music. Notation-first teaching buries those patterns under symbols, so you decode dots instead of learning how music actually works.

🎮

The app "fix" isn't playing either

Falling-note games remove notation but replace it with screen-following. Take the screen away and there's still no song. The answer isn't no structure. It's the right order of structure.

Music first. Reading through the music.

On the Ridley Method you play a real song from the very beginning, hands on keys, no notation required. Reading and theory arrive later, taught through the songs you already play, where every symbol finally has a sound attached. You end up doing both: playing and reading.

Playing without sheet music: the questions we hear most

Can you learn piano without reading sheet music?

Yes. You can play real songs without reading a note, and starting that way is faster for most adults. Reading comes later, taught through the songs you already play, so notation lands as names for things your hands already know.

Is it bad to learn without sheet music?

No, as long as reading isn't skipped forever. The traditional order (notation first, music later) stalls adults. The reverse order keeps momentum and still gets you reading. For one real example: Glenwood Clark started at 68 and in one year learned to play with both hands and read music.

How do you play piano without reading music?

Real songs are built from patterns: chords, progressions, and shapes that repeat across music. Learn the patterns inside songs you love and you can play long before you can sight-read. That's how Stephen learned on a street piano, and it's how the method starts.

Will I ever learn to read music?

Yes. Reading and theory arrive in the Musical Fluency phase, taught through your repertoire rather than through drills. Most students find notation far easier once they can already play.

Is playing by ear or reading music better?

They're not rivals; they're stages. Pattern-playing gets you making music now, and reading multiplies what you can learn later. A method built for adults sequences them in that order.

The best ways for adults to learn piano (ranked).

Most "best piano course" lists are written by people who've never taught an adult beginner. This one uses four criteria that actually matter if you're past 30 and serious:

  1. Time to your first real song (not an exercise, a song you'd play for someone)
  2. Whether you can play without the screen (the app-dependence test)
  3. Feedback and follow-through (the reason most adults quit isn't difficulty, it's drift)
  4. True cost over two years

For transparency: this guide is written by Ridley Academy, and yes, we rank ourselves first, on criteria we're happy to defend. We've kept the treatment of every other option factual and fair.

Best for: adults who want to actually play, not just practice.

No. 2

Private lessons

The classic route, and with the right teacher it works. The catch: quality varies enormously, most curricula are built for children working toward exams, and the cost compounds. Two years of weekly lessons at $50 to $100 runs $5,200 to $10,400.

Best for: learners who want in-person accountability and have a vetted adult-specialist teacher nearby.

No. 3

Simply Piano, Yousician, Flowkey

The apps are polished and the gamification is genuinely fun at first. The limitation shows up when the screen goes away: falling-note games train note-tracking, not musicianship, and most app streaks quietly die within months. Subscription costs also add up over the years.

Best for: testing whether you enjoy touching a keyboard at all.

No. 4

YouTube tutorials

There is genuinely great free teaching on YouTube. What it can't give you is sequence and feedback: without a path, adults binge tutorials and still can't play a full song. Works best as a supplement to a structured method.

Best for: supplementing a real curriculum, or zero-budget exploration.

No. 5

Method books on your own

Adult method books are thorough and inexpensive, but self-teaching from paper has the highest quit rate of anything on this list. No feedback, no sequence adjustments, no one noticing when you stall.

Best for: highly self-directed learners with prior music experience.

The honest bottom line

Every option on this list has made someone a pianist. The question is the odds. If you're an adult who has already tried an app or lessons and still can't sit down and play, the missing ingredient usually isn't information. It's a method built for adults plus someone keeping you on the path.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best way for an adult over 50 to learn piano?

A method that starts with real songs and includes feedback. Most of our students are over 50; adults often progress faster than kids because they bring more patience and a lifetime of songs they love. More in our piano after 50 guide.

Are piano apps worth it?

As a first taste, yes. As a path to real playing, they usually stall: they train you to follow the screen rather than to play. The test is simple: can you play a song with no screen in front of you?

How long does it take an adult to learn piano?

With the right method you can play your first real song in the first week and build real repertoire inside three months. Fluency is a 12-month arc.

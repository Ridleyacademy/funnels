Why the hourly model is expensive for adults

It's not that teachers overcharge. It's that the structure works against an adult learner in three ways.

⏳

You pay by the hour, forever

Hourly teaching has no finish line. The slower the method, the more lessons it takes, and theory-first sequencing is slow by design. The meter runs either way.

🎓

Adult prices, child's syllabus

Most curricula run on childhood method books and grade pieces. You pay adult rates to be walked toward an exam you'll never take, instead of toward the songs you actually want to play.

🗓️

One hour a week isn't a system

The other six days you're on your own. No path between lessons, no one hearing the practice, and progress resets every time life interrupts for a fortnight.

The alternative isn't cheaper lessons. It's a better method.

The Ridley Method reverses the order: real songs first, theory taught through the music you're playing, not before it, with mentorship for follow-through. When the path is shorter, the total cost of becoming a player changes, not just the rate.

Piano lesson costs: the questions we hear most

How much do piano lessons cost?

Weekly private lessons typically run $50 to $100 per lesson in most markets. Over two years of weekly lessons that adds up to $5,200 to $10,400, before books, recital fees, and travel time.

Are private lessons worth it for adults?

With the right teacher, they can be. The catch is that most lesson curricula are built for children working toward exams, so adults pay adult prices for a child's syllabus. Before committing thousands, make sure the method is built for what you actually want: playing real songs.

What's a cheaper alternative to a private teacher?

Apps are cheap but usually train screen-following rather than playing. A structured adult method with mentor feedback sits between the two: a real path and real feedback without paying by the hour.
